Land Degradation & Desertification

Land degradation happens when land loses its ability to support crops, forests or natural ecosystems, often due to deforestation, overgrazing, unsustainable farming and climate change. [web:66][web:88]

Under SDG 15, countries have committed to combat desertification, restore degraded land and soil, and strive for a land degradation‑neutral world by 2030. [web:1][web:61][web:72]

Key concepts

What is land degradation?

Long-term decline in soil health and land productivity, making it harder to grow food or support nature. [web:66][web:88]

What is desertification?

Land in dry areas becoming less productive because of human activities and climate change, not just “sand deserts”. [web:72][web:75]

Why it matters

It threatens food security, water resources, biodiversity and the livelihoods of millions of people. [web:9][web:65]

SDG 15.3 goal

By 2030, countries aim to combat desertification, restore degraded land and strive for land degradation neutrality. [web:1][web:61][web:72]

Causes and impacts

Main causes

  • Deforestation and removal of vegetation cover. [web:72][web:88]
  • Overgrazing and unsustainable farming practices. [web:72][web:88]
  • Urban expansion and infrastructure development on fertile land. [web:65][web:88]
  • Climate change, droughts and extreme weather events. [web:9][web:80]

Key impacts

  • Loss of soil fertility and lower crop yields. [web:88]
  • Greater risk of floods, dust storms and drought. [web:9][web:80]
  • Loss of habitats and biodiversity on land. [web:1][web:11]
  • Economic losses and pressure to migrate from affected areas. [web:9][web:80]
